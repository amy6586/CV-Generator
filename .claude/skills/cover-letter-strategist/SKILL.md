# Cover Letter Strategist — Amy Raygada

A skill for writing cover letters that feel human, direct, and specific — not like a CV summary in paragraph form.

## Golden rules

- Never invent projects, metrics, titles, or responsibilities not in master-cv.md or stated by Amy in the conversation.
- Voice rules: no em dashes, short sentences, direct and practitioner-grounded, no AI-sounding phrasing or corporate filler.
- A cover letter is not a CV rewrite. It answers: why this company, why this role, why Amy — in that order of specificity.
- Length: 3–4 tight paragraphs. Never more than one page. Never padding.
- Always read the JD fully before writing. Pull specific language from the JD to mirror back accurately (not flattery — alignment).

## Workflow

### 1. Extract what matters from the JD

- What problem is this company trying to solve by hiring for this role?
- What 2–3 things would make someone a stand-out candidate vs. a competent one?
- Is there a company mission, product, or context that Amy can engage with genuinely?

### 2. Match to master-cv.md

Find 2–3 specific, concrete pieces of evidence from Amy's career that directly address the above. These become the backbone of the letter — not themes, not adjectives, actual things she did.

### 3. Structure

**Paragraph 1 — The hook (3–4 sentences)**
Open with something specific about the company or the role that shows Amy has actually read the JD. Then connect it directly to what she does. No "I am writing to apply for..." openings.

**Paragraph 2 — The evidence (4–5 sentences)**
Two concrete examples from master-cv.md that show she has already done the core of what they need. Be specific: what the situation was, what she did, what the outcome was. No vague claims.

**Paragraph 3 — Why here, why now (3–4 sentences)**
What is genuinely interesting about this company or this moment for Amy. If there's nothing genuine to say, flag it — don't fabricate enthusiasm. This paragraph separates real applications from spray-and-pray ones.

**Paragraph 4 — Close (2–3 sentences)**
What Amy is asking for (a conversation, an interview). Direct, not deferential. No "I look forward to the opportunity to potentially discuss..."

### 4. Self-check before delivering

- Does every claim trace to master-cv.md or Amy's direct input?
- Does it sound like Amy wrote it, or like a template was filled in?
- Would a hiring manager at this company find it specific enough to be interesting?
- Is it under one page?

## Output

One cover letter, ready to send. Flag any section where Amy should personalise further (e.g. if she has a specific connection at the company, a genuine reason for interest beyond the JD, or a recent news item worth referencing).
