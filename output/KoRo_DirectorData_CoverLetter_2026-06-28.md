**Director Data, KoRo**

KoRo describes itself as a data company that happens to sell food. That framing caught my attention because it is the only honest way to describe what makes a modern D2C brand defensible at scale. The edge is not a single product. It is the engine behind it: the data model, the customer relationships, the test-and-learn loop. Building that engine is exactly what I do.

The closest thing in my career to what KoRo needs is what I built at SMG Swiss Marketplace Group: a multi-brand digital marketplace with D2C and retail signals, no unified data layer, and a team that routed everything through a central queue. I built the Data Product team from scratch, owned the full platform stack, and replaced the ticket model with self-service analytics that non-technical teams across four business units actually used. Data infrastructure utilisation and self-serve adoption went up 100%. Teams validated ideas without waiting for my team to do it for them. In parallel I have been leading a data strategy and platform engagement for a major European omnichannel retailer at Thoughtworks, working specifically on connecting online and in-store customer signals into a unified view. The problem KoRo is describing is one I have been solving in practice.

What draws me specifically to KoRo is the scale of the data asset and the clarity of the next problem. 2.5 million direct customer relationships, 50 attributes per customer, and a test-and-learn loop that validates online before scaling to retail is a genuinely interesting data challenge. I am also a KoRo customer, which means I have seen the product side of what the data engine is meant to support. Most companies at this stage either over-engineer a platform no one uses or under-invest until the data debt slows them down. The Director Data role is about getting that balance right, and that is the kind of work I find worth doing well.

I would welcome a conversation about the role and what the next 12 months look like for the data team at KoRo.
